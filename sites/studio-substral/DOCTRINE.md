# STUDIO SUBSTRAL
## Design & Experience Doctrine v1

### Status

CREATIVE DIRECTION LOCKED

This document defines the visual, interaction, motion and experience system for the Studio Substral website.

It is not a mood board.

It is not a loose reference.

Implementation should preserve this direction unless a technical, accessibility or performance constraint requires adaptation.

---

# 1. Brand Idea

**Studio Substral looks beneath the visible website to understand the systems determining whether it actually works.**

The website itself must demonstrate this philosophy.

The experience moves through:

**SURFACE → DECOMPOSITION → DIAGNOSIS → UNDERSTANDING → DESIGN → RECONSTRUCTION**

The site should feel like:

**luxury architecture practice × engineering laboratory × editorial design studio**

It must NOT feel like:

- SaaS;
- generic digital agency;
- AI startup;
- Webflow template;
- crypto company;
- developer portfolio;
- marketing funnel.

---

# 2. Core Statement

Primary hero:

# LOOK BENEATH
# THE SURFACE.

Supporting statement:

**Your website is working.
But is it working for you?**

Core philosophy:

> We measure what exists before deciding what should change.

Closing principle:

# WHAT'S UNDERNEATH
# DETERMINES WHAT HAPPENS ABOVE IT.

Primary CTA concept:

**Start with an assessment.**

Interactive assessment language:

# SEE WHAT WE SEE.

---

# 3. Brand Name

Formal name:

**Studio Substral**

Primary visual wordmark:

**SUBSTRAL**

STUDIO is secondary metadata.

Preferred treatments:

`SUBSTRAL`

with small:

`STUDIO / MANCHESTER, NH`

or:

`STUDIO SUBSTRAL`

in navigation/context where clarity is useful.

Do not create a decorative logo merely because brands conventionally have one.

Typography may be the primary identity.

---

# 4. Design Principle

Studio Substral is not selling decoration.

Therefore the website must not use decoration without purpose.

Every major visual element should support at least one of:

- hierarchy;
- understanding;
- evidence;
- navigation;
- interaction;
- atmosphere;
- narrative.

If an element exists only to make the page "look more designed," remove it.

---

# 5. Visual Character

The experience should communicate:

**precision**

**taste**

**intelligence**

**restraint**

**materiality**

**technical competence**

**confidence without spectacle**

The site may be visually dramatic.

It must never become visually noisy.

---

# 6. Palette

The palette is predominantly monochromatic.

## Substral Black

Warm charcoal.

Never default browser black.

Target character:

`#11110F` territory.

Final value may be tuned visually.

## Mineral

Warm architectural off-white.

Target character:

`#F0EDE5` territory.

Should feel like stone/paper rather than white UI.

## Graphite

Secondary dark material.

Used for dimensional surfaces and subtle hierarchy.

## Muted Structural Gray

For technical labels, measurement lines and secondary metadata.

## Accent

ONE restrained accent family.

Preferred territory:

**oxidized copper / mineral green / unusual muted metallic tone**

The accent is not decoration.

Use it for:

- active measurement;
- selected layer;
- focus;
- CTA interaction;
- diagnostic status;
- tiny technical details.

Do not create large accent backgrounds.

Do not introduce multiple competing brand colors.

---

# 7. Typography

Use two principal typographic voices.

## Editorial Grotesk

Purpose:

- hero statements;
- section declarations;
- major narrative;
- case-study titles;
- high-level philosophy.

Character:

- modern grotesk;
- strong at extreme scale;
- precise rather than friendly;
- excellent uppercase;
- not excessively geometric;
- not startup-soft.

Hero typography should feel architectural.

Large type may occupy substantial portions of the viewport.

## Technical Mono

Purpose:

- measurements;
- evidence classes;
- diagnostic labels;
- section numbering;
- metadata;
- coordinates;
- audit results;
- timestamps where appropriate.

Examples:

`01 / PERFORMANCE`

`MEASURED`

`LCP 4.8s`

`OBSERVED`

The mono voice represents evidence.

Do not overuse it for normal prose.

---

# 8. Typography Behavior

Headlines should generally be short.

Prefer:

**LOOK BENEATH
THE SURFACE.**

over:

**We create innovative digital experiences that transform your online presence.**

Never use marketing filler to occupy visual space.

Body copy should be editorial and readable.

Avoid stacking dozens of one-line statements merely for visual drama.

---

# 9. Spatial System

Use generous negative space.

Sections should breathe.

The site should feel composed rather than filled.

Avoid unnecessary containers.

Content should frequently exist directly within the spatial composition rather than inside cards.

Strong alignment is essential.

Use deliberate grids.

Asymmetry is welcome when controlled.

---

# 10. Forbidden Visual Patterns

DO NOT use:

- generic SaaS cards;
- dashboard-card grids as decoration;
- excessive rounded rectangles;
- glowing gradient blobs;
- neon cyberpunk gradients;
- glassmorphism everywhere;
- floating pill labels everywhere;
- giant gradient CTA panels;
- random particle backgrounds;
- animated mesh gradients;
- excessive drop shadows;
- generic laptop mockups;
- floating phones;
- stock photos of people pointing at screens;
- meaningless 3D spheres;
- orbiting logos;
- testimonial carousels;
- fake counters;
- fake metrics;
- excessive parallax;
- custom cursors that reduce usability;
- scroll hijacking.

Rounded corners may exist when the object genuinely calls for them.

They are not the default design language.

---

# 11. Signature Three.js Object

The central visual is an abstract website represented as a physical dimensional object.

It is NOT:

- a laptop;
- a browser screenshot floating in space;
- a generic UI card stack.

It should feel like an engineered object composed of physical layers.

Material inspiration:

- smoked acrylic;
- frosted translucent polymer;
- thin machined aluminum;
- architectural glass;
- graphite composite;
- subtle metallic edges.

Lighting should be cinematic but restrained.

No gaming aesthetic.

No chrome sci-fi object.

---

# 12. The Six Layers

The website decomposes into:

### 01 PERFORMANCE

How quickly and reliably does the experience become useful?

### 02 ACCESSIBILITY

Who can actually use it?

### 03 CONVERSION

Does attention have somewhere useful to go?

### 04 SEARCH

Can the right people discover it?

### 05 TRUST

Does the experience support confidence in the business behind it?

### 06 DESIGN

Only after understanding the above do we decide what the experience should become.

The ordering is intentional.

**DESIGN IS LAST.**

Do not reorder the layers for aesthetic reasons.

---

# 13. Motion Physics

Motion should feel:

**slow**

**precise**

**weighted**

**intentional**

Objects have mass.

Nothing bounces unnecessarily.

No elastic startup animation language.

No playful overshoot.

No constant ambient motion competing for attention.

Pointer response should be subtle.

Users should occasionally wonder whether their movement caused the dimensional shift.

That is preferable to obvious mouse-following effects.

---

# 14. Scroll Narrative

The homepage is not a collection of sections.

It is one narrative.

## ACT I — SURFACE

Dark environment.

SUBSTRAL wordmark.

Hero:

**LOOK BENEATH
THE SURFACE.**

Supporting:

**Your website is working.
But is it working for you?**

The assembled website object is visible.

Very subtle pointer parallax.

---

## ACT II — DECOMPOSITION

Scroll begins separating the dimensional object.

Layers move along depth.

Technical labels emerge.

Camera movement is restrained.

The user progresses through:

Performance
Accessibility
Conversion
Search
Trust
Design

Supporting philosophy appears:

> We measure what exists before deciding what should change.

---

## ACT III — DIAGNOSIS

Major visual transition.

Dark cinematic environment gives way to warm Mineral editorial space.

Large statement:

# WE DIAGNOSE
# BEFORE WE DESIGN.

Introduce:

**Discover → Diagnose → Advise**

Explain:

Sometimes the answer is a rebuild.

Sometimes the answer is targeted remediation.

Sometimes the existing website is doing its job.

Studio Substral should be willing to recommend all three.

---

## ACT IV — ASSESSMENT

Primary interactive moment.

# SEE WHAT WE SEE.

Large domain input.

Example:

`yourbusiness.com`

CTA:

**Analyze →**

Eventually powered by PulseForge Website Opportunity Intelligence.

The experience should distinguish:

### WHAT WE MEASURED

Deterministic technical evidence.

### WHAT WE OBSERVED

Direct structural/business observations.

### WHAT IT MAY MEAN

Bounded interpretation.

### WHAT WE'D INVESTIGATE NEXT

Advice.

Never turn this into:

`YOUR WEBSITE SCORE: 43/100`

followed by manufactured fear.

---

## ACT V — WORK

Case studies are editorial stories.

Do NOT create a generic portfolio grid.

Each major project should show:

**Context**

**Diagnosis**

**Decision**

**Experience**

**Outcome**

Use large visuals.

Allow individual projects to occupy substantial page space.

The first suitable case study may be MCFO Services if approved for public use.

---

## ACT VI — RECONSTRUCTION

Return gradually to the dark environment.

The six dimensional layers reappear separately.

As the user progresses, they converge.

Performance.

Accessibility.

Conversion.

Search.

Trust.

Design.

They align.

One final precise assembly movement.

The object becomes whole.

Closing statement:

# WHAT'S UNDERNEATH
# DETERMINES WHAT HAPPENS ABOVE IT.

CTA:

**Start with an assessment →**

End.

---

# 15. Discover → Diagnose → Advise

This is a foundational Studio Substral operating principle.

Do not prescribe before diagnosing.

The site should communicate that Studio Substral may conclude:

**NO REDESIGN REQUIRED**

when evidence supports that conclusion.

This increases credibility and differentiates the studio from design shops incentivized to recommend redesigns to everyone.

---

# 16. Assessment Integrity

The future assessment product must separate:

`MEASURED`

`OBSERVED`

`INFERRED`

`UNKNOWN`

Never use AI-generated assertions as deterministic findings.

Never manufacture urgency.

Never claim:

- revenue loss without evidence;
- customer abandonment without evidence;
- legal noncompliance from automated checks;
- guaranteed SEO impact;
- guaranteed conversion improvement.

Urgency comes from credible evidence.

---

# 17. Responsive Design

Mobile is NOT a reduced desktop screenshot.

Design intentionally for mobile.

The narrative remains:

Surface → Decomposition → Diagnosis → Assessment → Work → Reconstruction.

Three.js may simplify substantially.

Possible mobile treatment:

- reduced layer depth;
- fewer simultaneous objects;
- simplified lighting;
- static/pre-rendered fallback where necessary;
- touch-based layer progression.

Do not sacrifice mobile usability to preserve desktop spectacle.

---

# 18. Progressive Enhancement

Core content must work without WebGL.

Three.js enhances the narrative.

It does not own essential information.

If WebGL is unavailable:

- render an elegant static dimensional composition;
- preserve layer labels;
- preserve narrative;
- preserve assessment CTA;
- preserve case studies.

---

# 19. Reduced Motion

Respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

Reduced-motion users should receive:

- static or minimally transitioned dimensional states;
- no camera travel;
- no depth-based scroll animation;
- no continuous parallax.

The experience must remain visually intentional.

Reduced motion is not a degraded version.

---

# 20. Performance Doctrine

Studio Substral cannot criticize slow websites while shipping a slow website.

Performance is part of the brand.

Three.js assets must be aggressively optimized.

Requirements should eventually include explicit budgets for:

- JavaScript;
- textures;
- models;
- fonts;
- initial render;
- LCP;
- CLS;
- INP.

Lazy-load noncritical dimensional assets.

Do not block initial content on Three.js initialization.

The hero statement should render immediately.

---

# 21. Accessibility Doctrine

Accessibility is part of the product, not a compliance badge.

Requirements:

- semantic HTML;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- sufficient contrast;
- meaningful labels;
- accessible forms;
- reduced motion;
- screen-reader compatible narrative;
- decorative 3D excluded appropriately from accessibility tree;
- no essential information encoded solely in animation.

---

# 22. Interaction Doctrine

Interactions should feel deliberate.

Buttons:

Minimal.

Strong typography.

Clear hover/focus state.

No excessive animated fills.

Links should feel tactile but restrained.

Domain assessment input should feel like a primary instrument rather than a marketing form.

---

# 23. Copy Doctrine

Studio Substral speaks with:

**clarity**

**precision**

**confidence**

**restraint**

Avoid:

- hype;
- agency clichés;
- fake sophistication;
- "we're passionate about";
- "cutting-edge";
- "innovative solutions";
- "elevate your brand";
- "digital transformation";
- "unlock your potential";
- excessive rhetorical questions;
- AI-sounding aphorisms.

Prefer specific statements.

Example:

BAD:

> We craft cutting-edge digital experiences that elevate brands and drive meaningful results.

GOOD:

> We measure what exists before deciding what should change.

---

# 24. Brand Relationship to PulseForge

Studio Substral is its own customer-facing brand.

Do not make the website a PulseForge advertisement.

PulseForge may power:

- discovery;
- website intelligence;
- assessments;
- acquisition;
- qualification;
- internal prioritization.

That infrastructure remains behind the experience unless disclosure becomes useful.

PulseForge may become relevant to a Studio Substral client later when the client's problem extends beyond the website into customer acquisition.

That transition should occur through diagnosis, not upselling.

---

# 25. What Success Feels Like

Within the first ten seconds, a prospect should think:

**These people see websites differently.**

Within the first minute:

**They understand more than visual design.**

After the assessment:

**They found something useful before asking me for money.**

After viewing the work:

**I want my company to feel like this.**

---

# 26. Implementation Rule

The approved visual reference and this doctrine are the creative source of truth.

Do NOT translate this into:

"premium dark website."

Do NOT substitute generic component-library aesthetics.

Do NOT simplify away the narrative because a standard landing-page structure is easier to implement.

When technical constraints require adaptation:

1. preserve the concept;
2. preserve hierarchy;
3. preserve evidence integrity;
4. preserve performance/accessibility;
5. adapt implementation.

Do not adapt the concept to fit convenient implementation.

---

# 27. Final Creative Test

Before approving any implementation, ask:

### Does this feel like Studio Substral?

Not:

"Is this a nice website?"

Not:

"Does this look modern?"

Not:

"Is the animation impressive?"

The standard is:

> **Does the experience make the philosophy physically understandable?**

If not, it is not finished.
