Act II Decomposition

A website is not
one thing.

It is six systems occupying the same rectangle. Most of them are invisible to the person paying for it, and five of them decide whether the sixth one matters.

01 Performance

  1. 01 / Performance

    How quickly and reliably does the experience become useful?

    Speed is not a number in a report. It is the interval between intent and usefulness. We measure that interval on the connections and devices your customers actually have, then establish which part of the stack owns it: the server, the render path, the fonts, the images, or the third-party scripts nobody remembers adding.

    Measured
    LCP
    CLS
    INP
    Time to first byte
    Transfer weight
    Render-blocking requests

  2. 02 / Accessibility

    Who can actually use it?

    Automated checks find a minority of real barriers. We report what the tooling measured and what a person had to verify by hand, and we keep those two things apart. A site can pass every automated rule and still be unusable with a keyboard.

    Measured
    Contrast ratios
    Focus order
    Form labelling
    Landmark structure
    Alternative text coverage
    Target sizes

  3. 03 / Conversion

    Does attention have somewhere useful to go?

    Most sites do not fail at persuasion. They fail at routing. Someone arrives holding a specific intention and the page offers no unambiguous next action — or it offers one that costs more effort than the intention can carry.

    Observed
    Primary action per template
    Depth to contact
    Form field count
    Navigation clarity
    Phone and email availability

  4. 04 / Search

    Can the right people discover it?

    Discovery is structural before it is editorial. Whether a crawler can resolve, render and index a page is a technical fact. Whether the page answers what a person actually searched for is a judgement — and we label it as one rather than presenting it as a measurement.

    Measured
    Indexability
    Canonical integrity
    Sitemap and robots
    Title and description coverage
    Structured data validity

  5. 05 / Trust

    Does the experience support confidence in the business behind it?

    Someone deciding whether to spend money reads signals the business has stopped noticing: a certificate warning, a licence number that appears nowhere, a copyright year three years stale, a form that never confirms it was sent.

    Observed
    Transport security
    Contact verifiability
    Licence and credential visibility
    Content currency
    Submission confirmation

  6. 06 / Design

    Only after understanding the above do we decide what the experience should become.

    Design decides what the experience becomes. It cannot decide what constrains it. When design runs first it inherits problems it did not create and cannot see, which is how an expensive rebuild ends up performing like the site it replaced.

    Decided last
    Hierarchy
    Narrative
    Typography
    Materiality
    Interaction
    Restraint

Design is last.

The order is not stylistic. Each layer above design constrains what design is able to achieve, so each one is established first. A studio that reverses this order is guessing, and charging for the guess.

Core principle

We measure what exists before deciding what should change.

Act III Diagnosis

We diagnose
before we design.

A prescription written before an examination is a sales technique, not a professional opinion. The sequence below is the whole method, and it is deliberately capable of concluding that we should do nothing.

  1. 01 Discover

    Establish what exists.

    The live site, the pages that carry traffic, the technical surface, the commercial context around it. Nothing is diagnosed at this stage and nothing is recommended.

  2. 02 Diagnose

    Separate what is wrong from what is merely dated.

    Every finding is classified by how we know it, and the classification travels with the finding for the rest of the engagement. Measurements are not upgraded into conclusions on the way to the report.

  3. 03 Advise

    State the recommendation and its limits.

    What we would change, what we would leave alone, and what evidence would change our answer. The recommendation is allowed to be small.

Four conclusions Diagnosis classes

Redesign candidate

Material deficiencies across several layers, reinforcing each other. Repairing them individually would cost more than rebuilding the foundation properly once.

Targeted remediation

Specific, identified, bounded problems. The existing site is worth repairing rather than replacing, and we will tell you which repairs matter and which are cosmetic.

Healthy site

No material deficiency found. The site is doing its job. This is a real outcome, we report it plainly, and we do not follow it with a proposal.

Insufficient evidence

We could not gather enough to diagnose responsibly. We say so, and we say what we would need, rather than filling the gap with confident-sounding inference.

“No redesign required” is a conclusion we are willing to reach.

Sometimes the answer is a rebuild. Sometimes it is targeted remediation. Sometimes the existing website is doing its job and the constraint on the business is somewhere else entirely.

A studio paid only to redesign will find a reason to redesign. We would rather be the studio you call because the last answer we gave you was accurate.

Act IV Assessment

See what we see.

Enter a domain. We run the measurement pass, a person reviews the findings, and you receive the assessment by email. There is no instant score page, because an instant score would be the part we do not believe in.

Any form works — example.com or https://www.example.com/page

Measured

A value a machine produced, with the method and the time of measurement recorded. Reproducible by you.

Observed

A structural fact about the site or the business. Present or absent. Recorded without interpretation.

Inferred

Bounded interpretation, tied to the findings it derives from, and marked as interpretation wherever it appears.

Unknown

We could not establish it. Recorded as a gap rather than quietly omitted or filled in.

What we measured

Deterministic technical evidence. Load behaviour, accessibility checks, indexability, transport security. Each finding carries its value, its source and its timestamp.

What we observed

Direct structural and business observations. Whether a primary action exists. How many steps to reach a human. Whether the pages state what the business does and where it does it.

What it may mean

Interpretation, bounded and attributed to the evidence beneath it. This section is allowed to be short, and it is allowed to say that the evidence does not yet support a conclusion.

What we’d investigate next

The specific evidence that would raise or lower our confidence, what it would take to get it, and which of our findings it would overturn.

What the assessment will never produce

  • A score out of one hundred.
  • A figure for revenue you are losing, when we do not have your revenue data.
  • A claim that visitors are leaving because of your site, when we do not have your analytics.
  • A legal compliance verdict derived from automated accessibility checks.
  • A guarantee of search ranking or conversion improvement.
  • Urgency the evidence does not support.

Act V Work

Work, told as
a diagnosis.

Each engagement is written up the way it happened: what we found, what we decided, what we built, and what we can honestly claim about the result.

Anchor Cleaning

Commercial cleaning

Manchester, New Hampshire

Build — two-audience split

Context

Anchor Cleaning services professional offices — law firms, accounting practices, medical suites — across Greater Manchester. The site had to carry both paid and organic search traffic and convert one narrow audience: the person inside a small professional office who decides who cleans it.

Diagnosis

The constraint was not visual. It was routing and proof. A facility manager comparing vendors needs the service area, what a visit involves, and how to start — in that order. A quote form asking for square footage and frequency demands information the visitor does not have yet, so it collects nothing.

Two audiences were also competing for one page. Commercial contracts and residential home cleaning have different buyers, different language and different decisions, and a single page serving both serves neither.

Decision

Split the audiences: commercial at the root carrying one primary action, residential on its own page with its own vocabulary. Two pages, no blog, nothing to maintain that does not earn its maintenance. Static HTML with the stylesheet inlined, so the first paint does not wait on a second network round trip.

Experience

A photographic hero establishes the standard being sold — this buyer is purchasing consistency, and consistency has to look like something. Below it the page turns to warm paper, a brass rule system carrying the hierarchy, and one six-field form that confirms in place rather than redirecting to a thank-you page.

Local search structure is part of the build rather than a later addition: structured data for the service, an explicit service-area list, sitemap and robots.

Outcome

The site is instrumented. Session recording and form-submission events are live, and submissions write straight into the operator’s work queue rather than an inbox where they wait.

We are not publishing conversion figures yet. We publish outcome numbers when we have a full quarter of data and the client has approved the numbers — which is the same standard we hold other people’s case studies to.

Publication standard

A case study is published when the client has approved the material and every claim in it is verifiable. A second engagement is complete and waiting on that approval. It is not shown here until it has it.
