Act II Decomposition

A website isn’t
one thing.

A website is six systems working in the same space. Most stay invisible until one fails. Together, they decide whether the design can do its job.

    How quickly can people start using the site?

    A speed score doesn’t tell you why a site feels slow. We test on the kinds of devices and connections your customers use. Then we trace the delay to the server, page code, fonts, images, or third-party tools.

  2. 02 / Accessibility

    Who can actually use it?

    Automated tests catch only some barriers. We separate what the software found from what a person checked by hand. A site can pass an automated test and still fail for someone using a keyboard or screen reader.

    Measured
    Contrast ratios
    Focus order
    Form labelling
    Landmark structure

  3. 03 / Conversion

    Can people tell what to do next?

    Most sites don’t need more persuasion. They need a clearer path. A visitor arrives with a goal, but the page gives them no obvious next step — or asks for more effort than they are ready to give.

    Observed
    Primary action per template
    Depth to contact
    Form field count
    Navigation clarity
    Phone and email availability

  4. 04 / Search

    Can the right people discover it?

    Search visibility starts with the basics: can search engines reach, understand, and index the page? Then we ask whether the page answers what a real customer searched for. The first part is measurable. The second requires judgment, and we label it that way.

    Measured
    Pages search engines can index
    Canonical page setup
    Sitemap and robots
    Page titles and descriptions
    Structured data

  5. 05 / Trust

    Does the site give people a reason to trust the business?

    Customers notice warning signs a business may no longer see: a security alert, missing credentials, outdated details, or a form that never confirms it was sent. Each one makes the next step feel riskier.

    Observed
    Secure connection
    Verifiable contact details
    Licences and credentials
    Up-to-date content
    Form confirmation

  6. 06 / Design

    What should the site become once the other problems are understood?

    Design shapes the experience, but it can’t fix a problem we haven’t found. When design comes first, a costly rebuild can inherit the same weaknesses as the site it replaced.

Design is last.

The order is practical. Speed, accessibility, conversion, search, and trust all limit what design can achieve. We settle those questions first. Anything else is guesswork — and you’re the one paying for it.

Act III Diagnosis

We diagnose
before we design.

Recommending a redesign before looking at the site is selling, not diagnosing. We inspect first. The right answer might be a rebuild, one focused fix, or no website work at all.

  1. 01 Discover

    Understand the site as it is.

    We review the live site, the pages customers use, the technical setup, and the business it supports. At this stage, we gather facts. We don’t jump to a recommendation.

  2. 02 Diagnose

    Separate real problems from signs of age.

    Every finding shows how we know it: measured, observed, inferred, or still unknown. That label stays attached, so an opinion never gets presented as proof.

  3. 03 Advise

    Recommend only what the evidence supports.

    We explain what to change, what to leave alone, and what new evidence could change the answer. The right recommendation may be small.

Four possible conclusions

Redesign candidate

Several parts of the site are failing together. Fixing them one at a time would cost more and leave the foundation weak, so a rebuild is the sensible option.

Targeted fix

A small number of clear problems are holding the site back. The current site is worth keeping, so we fix those problems without replacing everything around them.

Healthy site

We found no serious problem. The site is doing its job, so the assessment ends without a redesign proposal.

Insufficient evidence

We couldn’t confirm enough to make a responsible recommendation. We explain what’s missing and what we’d need next, rather than guessing.

“No redesign required” is a conclusion we’re willing to reach.

The answer may be a rebuild, a focused fix, or no website work at all. The real problem may sit somewhere else in the business.

A studio that only sells redesigns will always find a reason to redesign. We’d rather give you an answer you can trust, even when that answer is to leave the site alone.

Act IV Evidence

Evidence before opinion.

We separate what we know from what we think. You can see what’s proven, what’s our judgment, and what’s still open. You’ll get a written assessment — not an instant score.

Measured

A value we can verify and reproduce.

Observed

A fact we can see directly in the site or business.

Inferred

A conclusion supported by the evidence, but still clearly marked as our interpretation.

Unknown

Something we couldn’t establish, so we record the gap instead of filling it in.

What we measured

Site speed, accessibility checks, search visibility, and security. Each result includes the value, its source, and when it was checked.

What we observed

What a person can confirm directly: whether the site has a clear next step, how hard it is to reach someone, and whether each page explains what the business does and where it works.

What it may mean

What the findings suggest and why. We tie every conclusion to the evidence behind it, and we say when the evidence isn’t strong enough.

What we’d investigate next

What we still need to learn, how we’d learn it, and which conclusion new information could change.

What the assessment will never produce

  • A score out of one hundred.
  • A revenue-loss estimate without your revenue data.
  • A claim that visitors are leaving because of the site without your analytics.
  • A legal compliance verdict based only on automated accessibility checks.
  • A guarantee of search ranking or conversion improvement.
  • Urgency the evidence doesn’t support.

Anchor Cleaning

Anchor Cleaning didn’t need more pages. It needed a clearer path for commercial buyers, visible proof, and a form that sent each request to the team responsible for it.

Problem

Decision
Separate commercial and home-cleaning paths
Build
Fast custom site with tracked forms
Evidence
Live forms and session recordings

Context

Anchor Cleaning serves law firms, accounting practices, medical offices, and other professional workplaces across Greater Manchester. The site needed to support paid and organic search, then guide one buyer: the person choosing an office cleaning company.

Diagnosis

The problem wasn’t the look. Buyers couldn’t quickly see the service area, what happens during a visit, or how to begin. The quote form asked for square footage and cleaning frequency before many visitors knew the answers.

The same page also spoke to commercial and home-cleaning customers. Those customers have different needs and make different decisions. Serving both on one page made both paths less clear.

Decision

Give commercial cleaning the main site and one clear action. Move home cleaning to its own page with language that fits that customer. Keep both pages lean. Build them as fast static pages so the first screen appears without waiting for extra files.

Experience

The main image shows the standard the company sells: a workplace that stays consistently clean. Below it, the page moves into a warmer editorial layout with clear proof and one six-field form. The form confirms the request on the page instead of sending the visitor elsewhere.

Search basics were built in from the start: clear service details, an explicit service-area list, structured data, a sitemap, and a robots file.

Outcome

Session recordings and form-submission tracking are live. New requests go straight to the team’s work queue, not an inbox where they can sit unseen.

We aren’t publishing conversion figures yet. We’ll publish them after a full quarter of data and client approval. That’s the standard we apply to every case study.

What we publish

We only publish a case study when the client approves it and every claim can be checked. A second project is complete but still awaiting approval, so it stays private.

Act VI Reconstruction

The work follows the evidence.

Not every site needs a rebuild. We recommend the smallest useful intervention that can solve the problem we found.

  1. Assessment

    Figure out what actually needs to change.

    A person reviews the live site across all six layers, then gives you a written diagnosis and a clear recommendation.

    Evidence reviewed · written diagnosis · priorities · next step

  2. Targeted fix

    Fix the part that is holding the site back.

    Focused technical, structural, or conversion work when the current site is worth keeping. We fix the identified problem without adding a visual overhaul you don’t need.

    Defined fixes · implementation · checks

  3. Redesign & build

    Rebuild the parts that need a new foundation.

    For sites where the message, page structure, copy, design, and technology are all working against each other. We rebuild them as one coherent site and check the result before launch.

    Strategy · design · build · launch checks

Best fit

Best for established businesses that already have a working site, but need help deciding what’s worth fixing, rebuilding, or leaving alone.

You may be considering a rebuild. The site may look credible but fail to generate enough qualified inquiries. Or one stubborn technical or structural problem may have survived several rounds of surface fixes.
